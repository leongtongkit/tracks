How to make trip-hop in your browser

By Tongkit Leong · November 18, 2026 · 7 min read

Trip-hop is a slow, heavy, moody style: a lazy drum break around 70 to 90 BPM, a deep sub bass, a dark looped chord or sample, and plenty of reverb. You can build all of it in the Tracks browser studio for free, with nothing to install.

What makes a track trip-hop

Trip-hop grew out of 1990s Bristol. It borrows hip-hop's breakbeats but slows them down and wraps them in atmosphere. The parts are few, and each one has a job:

  • A slow tempo. Roughly 70 to 90 BPM, with a heavy, dragging feel.
  • A lazy break. A fat kick, a snare on beats two and four, and sparse hi-hats played with swing.
  • A deep, simple bass. Long notes that move slowly under everything.
  • A dark loop. Minor chords or a chopped sample, rolled off on the top end.
  • Space. Long reverb and delay that make the track feel like a large, dim room.

Step 1: Set a slow tempo

Open the studio and set the BPM in the transport bar. Try 78 to start. At this speed every hit has room to ring out, so you can play fewer notes and let the silence do work. If you want a reference for other styles, see the BPM by genre cheat sheet.

Step 2: Program a lazy break

Add a drum track and open the drum machine. Put a kick on beat one and a snare on beats two and four. Add a second kick a little after beat three for a limping feel, then keep the hi-hats sparse, with a few ghosted hits instead of a steady stream.

Turn the swing up so the hats sit behind the grid. Trip-hop drums should sound slightly tired, not rigid. See Quantize and swing explained for how much to use. Lower the velocity on the soft hits in the piano roll so the pattern breathes.

A bigger, rounder kick suits this style. The 808 vs 909 guide explains which kit to start from.

Step 3: Add a deep sub bass

Add a synth track and choose a clean sine or a square wave with the filter closed down. Write a bass line in the piano roll with long notes, one or two per bar, following the root of your chords. Keep it simple: in a sparse track, a bass line that moves too much pulls attention away from the groove.

Make sure the bass and kick do not fight for the same moment. If they smear together, read how to lock a bassline to the kick.

Step 4: Build a dark, moody loop

You have two options, and both work.

Option A: play the chords

Add a pad or SoundFont electric piano and write a two-chord or four-chord loop in a minor key, such as a minor chord moving to the chord a whole step below. Hold the chords for a full bar each. The chord progressions guide shows how to draw them quickly.

Option B: chop a sample

Load a sound you have the rights to use into the 16-pad sampler, slice it, and rearrange the slices into a short loop. How to chop and flip a sample covers the steps. Be careful with copyright: use your own recordings or material you are cleared to use.

Either way, close down the high end with the track EQ so the loop sits behind the drums like an old record. A low-cut filter on the loop also stops it from crowding the sub bass.

Step 5: Add space and grit

Space is the signature of trip-hop. Create a reverb send and a delay send on the mixer, then feed the snare, the loop, and any lead sound into them. Use a long reverb tail on the snare so each hit blooms and fades. See reverb and delay explained for settings.

For dust, put a bitcrusher or a little saturation on the loop track and keep it subtle. Leave the kick and bass clean. Dirt on the low end makes a slow track muddy fast.

Add one odd detail

Trip-hop often has one strange element: a reversed note, a thin high melody, or a short vocal-like phrase. Add a single one, low in the mix, and let it appear only now and then.

Step 6: Arrange and leave room

Because the track is slow, sections can be short. A simple shape:

  1. Intro (4 bars): the loop alone, soaked in reverb.
  2. Verse (8 to 16 bars): drums and bass enter, loop continues.
  3. Break (4 bars): drop the drums and let the reverb tail ring.
  4. Return (8 to 16 bars): everything back, maybe with the odd detail added.
  5. Outro (4 bars): strip back to the loop.

See song structure and arrangement for more on building sections on the timeline.

Step 7: Mix and export

Set the kick and bass first, then bring in the loop until it supports them without covering them. Keep the whole mix a little dark. Check it in mono at least once: wide reverb can hide phase problems, and the mono check guide shows how.

When it sounds right at a low volume, export to WAV or MP3. The export guide has the details. Your project stays in your browser throughout. Tracks does not upload it anywhere, so use the project file export if you want a backup.

Frequently asked questions

What BPM is trip-hop?

Most trip-hop sits between 70 and 90 BPM. Some tracks use a half-time feel at a faster tempo, but the groove should still feel slow and heavy.

What is the difference between trip-hop and lo-fi hip-hop?

Both are slow and textured. Trip-hop is darker and more cinematic, with heavier bass, longer reverb, and minor-key moods. Lo-fi hip-hop is usually warmer and lighter, built for relaxed listening. For the lighter style, see make lo-fi hip-hop.

Do I need a sample to make trip-hop?

No. A pad or electric piano playing a minor chord loop, filtered and run through reverb, gets the same mood. Samples are optional.

How do I get that big, spacious sound?

Use sends, not inserts, for reverb and delay, make the tail long on the snare, and keep the dry parts sparse so the space is audible.

Can I make trip-hop for free in a browser?

Yes. Tracks includes the drum machine, synths, sampler, and mixer effects you need, and it is free with no account.
