A free Logic Pro alternative that runs in any browser

By Tongkit Leong · August 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Logic Pro is a one-time-purchase DAW that only installs on a Mac or an iPad. Tracks is a free browser DAW with a piano roll, built-in synths, drum machines, and a full mixer that opens in a tab on Windows, Mac, Chromebook, or Linux, with no purchase, no install, and no account.

The core difference: a Mac purchase vs. a tab

Logic Pro is Apple software. It's a one-time purchase from the Mac App Store, and it only runs on macOS or, in a separate mobile edition, on iPad — there is no Windows version and never has been. If your main computer isn't a Mac, Logic Pro isn't an option at all, no matter what you're willing to pay.

Tracks doesn't care what platform you're on. You open tracks.jfound.net in Chrome, Firefox, Edge, or Safari — on Windows, macOS, Linux, or a Chromebook — and every feature is available immediately, for free, with nothing gated behind a purchase or an app store account.

Where Logic Pro is still the stronger tool

Logic Pro's biggest advantage is depth built up over decades of Apple development: a massive built-in sound library of instruments and loops, the Alchemy sample-manipulation synth, an AI-assisted virtual drummer, Flex Time and Flex Pitch for reshaping audio after the fact, and Audio Unit (AU) plugin hosting, so you can load third-party instruments and effects from Apple's plugin ecosystem straight into a Logic session. None of that is available in a browser — Tracks does not support AU or VST plugin hosting, and its instrument library is built into the engine rather than downloadable content.

Logic Pro's Live Loops view is also a genuinely different way to work — launching audio and MIDI clips like a grid instead of arranging on a fixed timeline — and its MIDI editing and scoring tools go deeper than most DAWs, browser-based or not. If your workflow depends on AU plugins, Apple's sound library, or Logic-specific tools like Drummer, nothing in a browser replaces that.

Where Tracks is the better starting point

Cost and platform

Logic Pro is a one-time purchase, which is cheaper over time than a subscription DAW, but it's still a real cost, and it requires owning a Mac or iPad to begin with. Tracks costs nothing and runs on whatever device you already have with a modern browser — no Apple hardware requirement, no purchase decision to make before you've even tried it.

Instruments built in, no library to manage

Tracks ships a subtractive synth and an FM synth for building patches from oscillators and filters, 808/909-style drum machines, a 16-pad sampler, and SoundFont (.sf2) playback — see the best free VST alternative instruments in the browser for a full rundown. There's no multi-gigabyte sound library to download first; everything is available the moment the tab loads.

A comparable session view and piano roll

Logic Pro's Live Loops isn't unique to Logic — Tracks has its own session/clip-launch view for triggering ideas outside a fixed timeline, alongside a standard arrangement timeline. The Tracks piano roll supports velocity editing, quantize, swing, and a tempo map, covering the core of day-to-day melody and chord work, even without Logic's scoring-editor depth.

Mixing depth without a plugin chain

Every track in Tracks has a parametric EQ, compressor, gate, and de-esser built in, plus reverb and delay sends, group buses, sidechain ducking, and automation on any parameter — see how to mix a track: EQ, compression, and reverb basics. In Logic Pro, a deep per-track chain often means stacking multiple stock or third-party AU plugins. In Tracks, that chain is already sitting on every channel.

Recording, vocals, and stems

Tracks records mic input with count-in and take comping, and a MIDI keyboard over Web MIDI with no drivers to install. It includes built-in autotune, time-stretch/repitch, and stem extraction that splits an imported audio file into vocals, drums, bass, and other. Logic Pro can do all of this too through Flex Pitch and its own recording tools, but it's tied to owning a Mac in the first place.

Privacy and local storage

Tracks never uploads a project anywhere — everything, including recorded audio, stays on your device in local browser storage. Logic Pro projects live locally on your Mac too, so on that point the two are philosophically similar: neither needs cloud storage to work. See where your project actually lives for how Tracks' local-only storage works and how to back up a project file.

Which one should you use?

Choose Logic Pro if

You already own a Mac, you want Apple's full built-in sound library and tools like Drummer and Alchemy, or your workflow depends on AU plugins from outside Logic. It's also the better fit if you want a single one-time purchase that keeps working offline with no browser tab in the way.

Choose Tracks if

You don't own a Mac, you don't want to pay anything before you've even tried making a track, or you just want to start writing right now from whatever laptop or Chromebook is in front of you. Tracks covers the instruments, mixing, recording, and export workflow most producers actually use, and because it's local-only, your audio never leaves your device either way.

Frequently asked questions

Can I run Logic Pro on Windows?

No. Logic Pro is macOS and iPad only — Apple has never released a Windows version. If your main machine runs Windows or Linux, a browser DAW like Tracks is one of the few options that isn't blocked by the platform itself.

Can Tracks load AU or VST plugins like Logic Pro can?

No. Browsers don't support hosting native AU or VST plugins, so Tracks — like any browser DAW — ships its instruments and effects built into the engine instead. If a specific Logic-only tool like Alchemy or Drummer is central to your sound, Logic Pro is the only path to using it.

Does Tracks have anything like Logic Pro's Live Loops?

Yes — Tracks has its own session view for launching clips outside a fixed timeline, alongside a standard multi-track arrangement view, so you can sketch ideas the same loop-based way before committing them to a timeline.

Can I move a project between Logic Pro and Tracks?

Not directly — Logic Pro project files and Tracks' .tracks.json project files are different formats, and neither reads the other's native file. You can bridge between them with standard interchange formats: export a standard MIDI file from Tracks (see how to export a MIDI file) to bring notes into Logic, or export stems or a WAV/MP3 mixdown to move finished audio the other way.

Is Tracks good enough to finish a full track without Logic Pro?

Yes. Tracks covers arrangement, instruments, recording, mixing, mastering-level loudness tools, and WAV/MP3/stem/MIDI export — the full path from a blank timeline to a finished, exported song, as covered in how to make a song in your browser for beginners. Whether it's "enough" depends on whether you need Logic-specific tools like AU plugin hosting or Apple's built-in sound library, which Tracks doesn't replace.
