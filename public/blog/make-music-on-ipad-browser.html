Make music on an iPad without downloading an app

By Tongkit Leong · September 4, 2026 · 7 min read

You can make a full song on an iPad without touching the App Store: open tracks.jfound.net in Safari or Chrome and you get a multi-track studio with synths, drum machines, a mixer, and mic recording, running entirely as a web page. Tracks treats a tablet the same as a laptop — the one device it will not open on is a phone.

Why a browser studio makes sense on a tablet

Most serious music apps on iPad are paid, tied to the App Store, and locked to iOS — nothing from GarageBand carries over if you switch to an Android tablet or a Chromebook. A browser studio sidesteps that entirely. Tracks is a website. If your tablet has a modern browser, it works, and the same project opens identically whether you are on an iPad, a Samsung Galaxy Tab, or a laptop later that day.

There is also nothing to manage. No App Store account, no update prompts, no storage taken up by an installed app. You navigate to the URL and the studio loads in the tab.

What actually works on a tablet screen

Tracks is built for a device with enough screen space to show a timeline, a piano roll, and a mixer without everything collapsing into menus — which is exactly the category a tablet sits in. The studio detects screen size and input type, and it treats any tablet-sized touchscreen the same as a desktop or laptop: the full interface loads, not a stripped-down version.

The one device Tracks intentionally blocks is a phone. A phone screen is too small to fit a multi-track arrangement view, a piano roll, and mixer controls at a usable size, so opening the studio on a phone shows a short message pointing you to a bigger screen instead of a broken layout. An iPad, an Android tablet, a Chromebook, or a laptop all pass that check and load the real studio.

On a tablet, most controls respond to touch the same way they respond to a mouse click: tap a step in the drum sequencer to toggle it, tap and drag in the piano roll to draw a note, tap a fader and drag to set a level. Fine-grained editing — dragging a short note or nudging an automation point by a pixel — is easier with a stylus or a connected trackpad than with a fingertip, but nothing about the studio requires either one.

Setting up your first session

Open Safari or Chrome on your tablet and go to tracks.jfound.net/studio. There is no sign-up screen — the studio loads straight into an empty project. If you have never made a song in Tracks before, the beginner's guide to making a song walks through setting a tempo, adding tracks, and building out an arrangement from nothing.

Your project saves to the browser's local storage automatically as you work, so closing the tab and reopening it later picks up where you left off, as long as you use the same browser on the same device. Tracks never uploads your project anywhere — there is no cloud sync between devices, so if you want to keep working on the same song on a different tablet or a laptop, export it as a .tracks.json file from the File menu first and reimport it on the other device.

Programming beats and melodies with touch

The drum machine is the fastest way to get sound out of a tablet session. Add a drum track, open the step sequencer, and tap the grid squares you want active — each row is a different drum sound, and the pattern loops so you hear your changes immediately. The full mechanics of the step sequencer, including velocity and swing, are covered in free online drum machine: program beats with no software.

For melodies and chords, add an instrument track and open the piano roll. Tap to place a note, and drag its edges to change its length. If you do not read music, you do not need to — the no-theory guide to writing a melody covers a practical way to find notes that work together by ear alone.

If you connect a MIDI keyboard, some tablet browsers support the Web MIDI API used for MIDI input, though support varies more by browser than it does on desktop — check that your specific browser and tablet combination lists Web MIDI support before relying on it for a session, and fall back to tapping notes into the piano roll if it is not available.

Recording vocals or an instrument

To record, add an audio track and tap the mic icon to arm it. Your tablet will prompt for microphone permission the first time — allow it, and that permission stays granted for the site on that browser. Set your input level so the meter sits in the green-to-yellow range without hitting red, then press Record in the transport.

A tablet's built-in mic works for quick ideas, but it picks up more room noise and handling noise than a dedicated microphone. If your tablet supports USB or a USB-C audio interface, plugging one in generally gives a cleaner signal than the built-in mic. Once you have a take, Tracks' built-in autotune can correct pitch — set the key and scale on the vocal track's effect chain and adjust the Speed dial for anything from a subtle correction to the fully robotic effect.

Mixing on a smaller screen

Open the Mixer view to see channel strips for every track. On a tablet, the mixer scales down to fit but keeps every control tappable: volume faders, pan, and the EQ and compressor on each channel. Work through tracks one at a time rather than trying to eyeball the whole mix at once — pull down anything that is too loud, pan wide elements away from center, and use EQ to carve out space where two instruments clash in the same frequency range.

For a fuller walkthrough of EQ, compression, and reverb basics, see how to mix a track: EQ, compression, and reverb basics.

Exporting your finished song

When the track is done, use File > Export and choose MP3 or WAV. The file downloads to your tablet the same way any other download does — check your browser's downloads or the Files app on iPad. From there you can share it through AirDrop, a messaging app, cloud storage, or upload it wherever you need. MP3 is the right pick for sharing; WAV keeps full quality if you plan to hand it off for further mixing or mastering. The difference between the two is covered in detail in how to export your song to MP3 or WAV.

Frequently asked questions

Does Tracks work on an iPad?

Yes. Tracks loads the full studio interface on an iPad in Safari or Chrome, the same way it loads on a laptop. It only blocks phone-sized screens, and an iPad is well above that threshold.

Do I need to install anything from the App Store?

No. Tracks is a website, not an app. There is nothing to download or install, and no App Store account is involved.

Does it work on Android tablets too?

Yes. Tracks checks screen size and input type, not the operating system, so an Android tablet with a modern browser gets the same full studio as an iPad.

Why does Tracks block phones but allow tablets?

The interface needs enough room to show a timeline, a piano roll, and a mixer at a usable size at the same time. A tablet screen is large enough for that; a phone screen is not. Rather than force a cramped, hard-to-use layout onto a phone, Tracks shows a short message asking you to switch to a bigger screen.

Can I use a stylus with Tracks on an iPad?

A stylus works for anything you would otherwise tap, and it gives you more precision for small edits like nudging a note in the piano roll or adjusting an automation point. It is not required — a finger works for everything, a stylus just makes fine detail work easier.

Will my project sync between my iPad and my laptop?

Not automatically. Tracks saves projects to local browser storage on the device you are using, and nothing is uploaded to a server. To move a project between devices, export it as a .tracks.json file on one device and import it on the other.
