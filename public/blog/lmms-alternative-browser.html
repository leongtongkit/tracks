A free LMMS alternative that runs in your browser

By Tongkit Leong · November 25, 2026 · 6 min read

LMMS is a free, open-source desktop app for making beats on Windows, Mac, and Linux. Tracks is a free alternative that runs in a browser tab, with built-in synths, drum machines, a mixer, and microphone recording, and no install or account.

The core difference: a free desktop app vs. a free tab

Both cost nothing, so price is not the deciding factor here. LMMS is software you download and install on a computer. It is built around a pattern-based workflow for composing electronic music and beats.

Tracks has nothing to install. You open freetracks.app in a modern browser on Windows, Mac, Linux, or a Chromebook and start. Projects stay on your device, and nothing is uploaded.

Where LMMS is the stronger tool

LMMS is a long-running open-source project, and it works offline once installed. That matters if you want a desktop program that does not depend on a browser, or if you want to read and modify the source code.

It can also load external plugins on some platforms and builds, which Tracks cannot do: browsers do not host native VST or AU plugins, so Tracks ships its instruments and effects built in. If your sound depends on a specific third-party plugin, check how your LMMS build handles it, and expect a desktop app to be the better fit.

Where Tracks is the better starting point

No download, works on any device

You can start on a school laptop, a Chromebook, or a borrowed computer where you cannot install software. Open the page and the studio is ready. See free online music maker for students and Chromebooks for that setup.

Recording is built in

Tracks records from a microphone with a count-in, takes comping for choosing the best take, and a MIDI keyboard over Web MIDI. If you want to sing or play over a beat, read how to record vocals in your browser.

Instruments are already inside

A subtractive synth, an FM synth, 808/909-style drum machines, a 16-pad sampler, and SoundFont (.sf2) playback are all included. For what each one does, see the best free VST alternative instruments in the browser.

A mixer for the everyday jobs

Each track has a parametric EQ, compressor, gate, and de-esser, plus reverb and delay sends, group buses, sidechain ducking, and automation. Export WAV, MP3, per-track stems, a MIDI file, or a project file.

Which one should you use?

Choose LMMS if

You want an offline desktop app, you value open-source software you can inspect, or you want to experiment with external plugins on your own machine.

Choose Tracks if

You want to start in seconds on any computer, record your own voice or instrument, and mix and export without installing anything. To see that path end to end, read how to make a song in your browser for beginners.

Frequently asked questions

Is LMMS really free?

Yes. LMMS is free and open source. Tracks is also free, with no trial period and no paid tier.

Can Tracks load VST plugins like some LMMS builds?

No. Browsers cannot host native VST plugins, so Tracks ships its instruments and effects built in.

Can I move a project between LMMS and Tracks?

Not directly, since the project formats differ. Export a standard MIDI file from Tracks (see how to export a MIDI file) to bring notes into another app, or import a MIDI file into Tracks. Stems or a WAV mixdown carry finished audio.

Does my music get uploaded?

No. Tracks is local-only: your projects stay in your browser on your device, so export a project file to back them up.

Can I finish a full song in Tracks?

Yes. You can arrange, record, mix, and export a finished song without leaving the tab.
