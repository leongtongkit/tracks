How to make neo-soul in your browser

By Tongkit Leong · November 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Neo-soul is built on rich extended chords, a loose groove that sits slightly behind the beat, and a bass line that sings. You can make all of it in the Tracks browser studio for free, with nothing to install.

What makes a track neo-soul

Neo-soul mixes soul, jazz, and hip-hop. It feels relaxed and human, and the harmony does most of the talking:

  • Extended chords. Sevenths, ninths, and elevenths instead of plain triads.
  • A loose groove. Drums with swing, soft ghost notes, and a snare that lands a touch late.
  • A melodic bass. A line that moves between chord tones instead of repeating one note.
  • A warm sound. Electric piano, soft pads, and a mix that never feels harsh.

Step 1: Set a relaxed tempo

Open the studio and set the BPM between 70 and 95. Around 80 is a good start. At this speed there is space for chords to ring and for the groove to breathe. The BPM by genre cheat sheet lists tempos for other styles.

Step 2: Write extended chords

Add a track with an electric piano from the SoundFont instruments and open the piano roll. Instead of three-note triads, stack four or five notes: root, third, fifth, seventh, and a ninth on top. A minor seventh chord with a ninth sounds instantly warmer than a plain minor chord.

Neo-soul progressions often change chords every one or two beats and move in unexpected steps. Try four chords over two bars, then repeat. Keep the top notes close together between chords so the voicing glides instead of jumping. The chord progressions guide shows how to draw them quickly, and the scales guide helps you stay in key.

Lower the velocity on some chord notes and leave the top note louder. That small difference makes the keys sound played, not pasted.

Step 3: Program a loose groove

Add a drum track and open the drum machine. Start with a simple pattern: kick on beat one, a second kick a little before beat three, and the snare on two and four. Then add soft ghost notes on the snare and a hi-hat pattern with varied velocity.

The feel comes from timing. Raise the swing amount and nudge the snare slightly late in the piano roll, so the backbeat drags a little. Do not quantize everything hard. Quantize and swing explained covers how far to go, and humanize MIDI adds small timing and velocity shifts for you. For the ghost notes, see ghost notes and snare groove.

Step 4: Add a melodic bass

Add a synth track with a round, low-passed bass sound. Write a line that follows the root of each chord, then add passing notes: a step up, a short slide of notes into the next chord, a rest. Neo-soul bass is almost a second melody, so leave gaps and let it talk.

Check that the bass and kick do not pile up on the same beat. If the low end feels crowded, read how to lock a bassline to the kick.

Step 5: Add layers and space

Add a soft pad playing the root and fifth of each chord, or a second keys part an octave up with a quiet, simple rhythm. Keep extra parts low in the mix so the electric piano stays at the center.

Create a reverb send on the mixer and feed it a little of the keys and the snare. Use a short to medium tail so the space adds warmth without washing out the groove. See reverb and delay explained for settings.

Leave room for a vocal or lead

Even an instrumental needs a gap where a voice or lead could sit. Keep the middle of the chords simple in one section, and the track will feel open. If you plan to sing over it, recording vocals in the browser walks through the setup.

Step 6: Mix warm and export

Set the faders first: drums and bass at a steady level, keys a little above, pads underneath. Use the track EQ to roll off harsh highs on the keys and drums, and a gentle compressor on the bass to even out its level. A touch of saturation can add warmth. See balance with faders first for the order.

When it sounds right at a low volume, export to WAV or MP3 using the export guide. Your project stays in your browser. Tracks does not upload it anywhere, so export the project file if you want a backup.

Frequently asked questions

What BPM is neo-soul?

Most neo-soul sits between 70 and 95 BPM. The groove matters more than the number: a slightly late snare and light swing make a slow tempo feel alive.

What chords are used in neo-soul?

Minor seventh, major seventh, ninth, and eleventh chords are the core. Add a ninth or eleventh on top of a seventh chord to get the classic lush sound.

Do I need to know music theory?

No. Draw four-note chords in the piano roll, listen, and move notes by a step until they sound good. The melody guide shows the same approach for single notes.

How is neo-soul different from lo-fi hip-hop?

Both are relaxed. Neo-soul leans on richer harmony and a live-band feel, while lo-fi hip-hop leans on dusty loops and a steady, simple pattern. See make lo-fi hip-hop for the other style.

Can I make neo-soul for free in a browser?

Yes. Tracks includes the electric piano and bass sounds, drum machine, and mixer effects you need, and it is free with no account.
