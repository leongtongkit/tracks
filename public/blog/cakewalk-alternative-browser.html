A free Cakewalk alternative that runs in your browser

By Tongkit Leong · November 26, 2026 · 6 min read

Cakewalk is a free, full-featured desktop DAW, but it only runs on Windows. Tracks is a free alternative that runs in a browser tab on Windows, Mac, Linux, and Chromebooks, with built-in synths, drum machines, a mixer, and microphone recording, and no install or account.

The core difference: a Windows program vs. a tab on any device

Both cost nothing, so price is not what decides this. Cakewalk is a large desktop application you download and install on a Windows PC. Tracks has nothing to install: you open freetracks.app in a modern browser and start.

If you use a Mac, a Linux machine, or a Chromebook, Cakewalk is simply not an option, and that is the most common reason people look for something else.

Where Cakewalk is the stronger tool

Cakewalk is a traditional, deep desktop DAW. It works offline, and it can host third-party VST plugins, so if your sound depends on a specific plugin you already own, a desktop DAW is the right home for it. Tracks cannot do that: browsers do not host native VST or AU plugins, so Tracks ships its instruments and effects built in.

A desktop DAW also suits big sessions with many tracks and heavy plugin chains, where a native app has more headroom than a browser tab.

Where Tracks is the better starting point

It runs wherever you have a browser

Start on a school laptop, a Chromebook, a Mac, or a computer where you cannot install software. See free online music maker for students and Chromebooks for that setup.

Recording is built in

Tracks records from a microphone with a count-in and takes comping for choosing the best take, and it accepts a MIDI keyboard over Web MIDI. To sing over a beat, read how to record vocals in your browser.

Instruments are already inside

A subtractive synth, an FM synth, 808/909-style drum machines, a 16-pad sampler, and SoundFont (.sf2) playback are included. For what each one does, see the best free VST alternative instruments in the browser.

A mixer for the everyday jobs

Each track has a parametric EQ, compressor, gate, and de-esser, plus reverb and delay sends, group buses, sidechain ducking, and automation. You can also use built-in autotune, time-stretch, and stem extraction. Export WAV, MP3, per-track stems, a MIDI file, or a project file.

Which one should you use?

Choose Cakewalk if

You are on Windows, you want an offline desktop DAW, and you rely on third-party VST plugins or very large sessions.

Choose Tracks if

You are on a Mac, Linux, or Chromebook, or you just want to start in seconds without installing anything. You can record, mix, and export a finished song in one tab. To see that path end to end, read how to make a song in your browser for beginners.

Frequently asked questions

Is Cakewalk really free?

Yes. Cakewalk is free to download for Windows. Tracks is also free, with no trial period and no paid tier.

Is there a Cakewalk for Mac?

No, Cakewalk is Windows-only. Tracks runs in the browser, so it works on a Mac as well as Linux and Chromebooks.

Can Tracks load VST plugins?

No. Browsers cannot host native VST plugins, so Tracks ships its instruments and effects built in.

Can I move a project from Cakewalk to Tracks?

Not directly, since the project formats differ. Export a MIDI file from Cakewalk and import it into Tracks (see how to import a MIDI file), or bring in rendered audio.

Does my music get uploaded?

No. Tracks is local-only: your projects stay in your browser on your device, so export a project file to back them up.
