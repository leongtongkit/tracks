How to fix a muddy mix: clear up low-mid buildup in your browser

By Tongkit Leong · October 7, 2026 · 6 min read

A muddy mix is almost always too much energy stacked up between roughly 200 and 400 Hz, where bass, kick, vocals, guitars and pads all overlap. Fix it by high-passing every track that does not need low end, cutting a few dB at the crowded frequency, and giving the kick and bass different jobs, all of which you can do with the per-track EQ in Tracks.

Why mixes get muddy

Almost every instrument has some energy in the low-mids, so the more tracks you add, the more that region builds up. No single track sounds wrong on its own. Together they turn the mix cloudy, boxy and hard to hear clearly, even at a loud volume.

The cause is usually overlap, not a bad sound. Fixing it means making room, not making everything quieter.

Step 1: find the muddy range

Open the EQ on a track you suspect, such as a pad, a piano or a vocal. Boost one band by about 6 dB with a fairly narrow Q, then sweep it slowly between 200 and 400 Hz while the full mix plays. When the sound turns boxy or cloudy, you have found the buildup. Reset the boost and note the frequency.

Do this on a few tracks. The offenders are usually the ones with the most sustained, full-bodied sound.

Step 2: high-pass what does not need low end

The fastest fix is also the most effective. Vocals, hi-hats, guitars, synth leads and pads rarely need anything below about 80 to 120 Hz, but they often carry it anyway. Switch on the high-pass filter in the track EQ and raise it until the sound just starts to thin, then back off a little.

Ten tracks each leaking a little rumble add up to a lot of mud. Removing it leaves the kick and bass to own the bottom. See the EQ cheat sheet by instrument for starting frequencies.

Step 3: cut, do not boost

Once you know the muddy frequency, make a gentle cut of 2 to 4 dB with a medium-wide Q on the tracks that crowd it most. Cutting mud from a pad or a guitar lets the vocal and the bass come through without touching their levels.

  • Pads and keys. Usually the biggest contributors. Cut first here.
  • Guitars and synth chords. A small cut near 250 to 350 Hz often opens the mix.
  • Vocals. Cut lightly if the voice sounds boxy, but be careful not to thin it out.

Step 4: separate the kick and the bass

If the low end itself is muddy, the kick and bass are probably fighting over the same notes. Decide which owns the sub and trim the other in that range. You can also duck the bass when the kick hits, which is covered in sidechain compression explained, and write the bass so it lands between kick hits, as in how to make a bassline that locks with the kick.

Step 5: thin the arrangement and check

Sometimes EQ is not the answer. If three layers all play the same chord in the same octave, mute one or move it up an octave. Fewer sounds in the low-mids is a free fix.

Then compare against a commercial track at the same volume, as described in how to mix with a reference track. If your mix still sounds cloudier, go back to steps 2 and 3.

Common mistakes

  • Boosting highs to compensate. Brightening a muddy mix hides the problem and gets fatiguing. Remove the mud instead.
  • Cutting too much. Cutting everything below 300 Hz makes the mix thin. Cut a little on the crowded tracks, not all of them.
  • Judging solo. A pad can sound great on its own and still be the cause of mud. Always check in the full mix.
  • Mixing too loud. Loud playback exaggerates bass. Check at a moderate volume.

Frequently asked questions

What frequency range is mud?

Usually about 200 to 400 Hz, though the exact spot depends on the tracks. Sweep a narrow boost to find it by ear, rather than trusting a fixed number.

Should I high-pass every track?

Every track except the ones that supply low end, such as the kick, bass and 808. Even those can take a gentle filter below the lowest note they play.

Can I fix mud with a compressor?

Not directly. Compression changes dynamics, not tone. Use EQ to remove the buildup, and compression afterward if you need control. See how to mix a track: EQ, compression, and reverb basics.

Can I do this in the Tracks browser studio?
