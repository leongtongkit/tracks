How to make city pop in your browser

By Tongkit Leong · November 16, 2026 · 7 min read

City pop is a glossy, danceable style built on a steady 4/4 beat, a melodic funky bass, and lush seventh and ninth chords on electric piano. You can write all of it in the Tracks browser studio for free, with no install and no account.

What makes city pop sound like city pop

  • Rich chords. Major seventh, minor seventh, and ninth chords give it a smooth, sunny-but-wistful color.
  • A moving bass. The bass plays a real melody under the chords instead of sitting on the root.
  • A steady, clean groove. Mid-tempo drums with a firm backbeat and tidy hi-hats.
  • A big hook. A clear, singable melody on top.
  • A polished mix. Warm, bright, and a little shiny, with chorus and short reverb.

Tracks has what you need: a piano roll with velocity, 808 and 909 drum machines, a subtractive and FM synth, SoundFont (.sf2) playback for electric piano and vibraphone, and a mixer with EQ, compression, chorus, and reverb.

Step 1: Set the tempo and feel

Open the studio and set the BPM between 100 and 120. That is fast enough to dance to and slow enough for the chords to breathe. Set the grid to 1/16. A touch of swing makes the groove relaxed, and the quantize and swing guide explains the setting.

Step 2: Program a steady drum beat

Open the drum machine and build a one-bar pattern:

  • Kick: on beats 1 and 3, with an extra hit just before beat 3 if you want some push.
  • Snare: on beats 2 and 4.
  • Closed hi-hat: on every eighth note, with the off-beats slightly quieter.
  • Open hi-hat: once at the end of every second bar.

Keep it clean. City pop drums support the chords instead of competing with them. For small variations, see hi-hat patterns.

Step 3: Write the chords

Add an electric piano track using a SoundFont. In the piano roll, write one or two chords per bar. A reliable four-bar loop in C major is Fmaj7, Em7, Dm9, G7. Play each chord as a block of four or five notes, and keep the voicing close together instead of jumping around the keyboard.

For a sweeter turn, replace a chord with a borrowed one, such as Fm6 before returning to C. If you want more chord ideas, read chord progressions in the piano roll.

Step 4: Add a funky bass line

Add a bass track and write a line that follows the chord roots but moves between them. Use short notes, a jump of an octave, and a quick run into the next chord. Leave small rests so the bass breathes. If it fights the kick, read how to lock a bassline with the kick.

Step 5: Add a lead melody and a key change

Write a simple melody on a bright synth or vibraphone. Stay mostly on the notes of the scale and repeat a short phrase twice, then change the ending. Repetition is what makes a hook stick, as covered in writing a catchy hook.

City pop is known for lifting the final chorus. Copy the last section and move all its notes up a whole step, or use the transpose tool as described in how to change the key.

Step 6: Mix for a warm, glossy sound

  1. Set the faders first: drums and bass balanced, chords and lead above the background parts.
  2. Add a high-pass filter on the electric piano so it leaves the low end to the bass.
  3. Add a little chorus to the chords for width, and a short, bright reverb on the lead.
  4. Gently compress the drums and bass so they stay even.

When you are happy, export a WAV or MP3. The steps are in How to export your song to MP3 or WAV. Your project stays in your browser the whole time, and Tracks never uploads it.

Frequently asked questions

What tempo is city pop?

Most city pop sits between 100 and 120 BPM in 4/4, though ballads go slower.

What chords are used in city pop?

Major seventh, minor seventh, dominant seventh, and ninth chords, often in progressions like Fmaj7, Em7, Dm9, G7.

Do I need a real bass or electric piano?

No. You can write both in the piano roll and play them with SoundFont instruments. Good note lengths and velocity matter more than the exact sound.

Can I make a key change in Tracks?

Yes. Copy the last section and transpose its notes up in the piano roll.
