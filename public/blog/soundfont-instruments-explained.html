SoundFont instruments explained: real instrument sounds in your browser

By Tongkit Leong · July 26, 2026 · 7 min read

A SoundFont (.sf2) is a bank of sampled real instruments — piano, strings, brass, organ, and more — that you load straight into a track in the Tracks browser studio and play like any other instrument. No plugin to install: pick a .sf2 file from your device, choose a preset, and write notes in the piano roll.

What a SoundFont actually is

A SoundFont is a file format, not a piece of software — it's a container of recorded samples plus the metadata that tells a playback engine how to use them: which sample plays at which pitch, how it loops while a note is held, how velocity affects tone, and how the sample fades out on release. One .sf2 file can hold a single instrument or an entire General MIDI bank of 128 — pianos, guitars, strings, brass, organs, synth pads, even a drum kit mapped across the keyboard.

The format has been around since the 1990s and is completely open, which is why it never went away. Whole communities of musicians and game composers have built and shared free .sf2 banks for decades, so there's a large supply of usable, no-cost sound sources outside anything a DAW ships with. A widely used example is FluidR3 GM, a free General MIDI bank that shows up bundled with several open-source audio tools — it's a reasonable place to start if you don't already have a .sf2 file on hand.

How SoundFont tracks work in Tracks

Tracks doesn't ship its own library of SoundFont banks — that's an important distinction from the built-in synths and drum machines, which need nothing extra. SoundFont playback is a way to bring your own sample banks into the studio and have Tracks play them back accurately, entirely in the browser, using the Web Audio engine.

Here's what actually happens when you use it:

  • You add a SoundFont track, which is its own track type alongside synth, drums, sampler, and audio tracks.
  • You load a .sf2 file from your device. Tracks parses it and stores the raw bytes locally in your browser — nothing is uploaded anywhere.
  • A preset dropdown lists every instrument the bank contains, labeled by bank and program number plus its name, so you can pick the one you want to play on that track.
  • Once a preset is selected, the track behaves like any other instrument track: write notes in the piano roll, play it live from a MIDI keyboard, and mix it with EQ, compression, and sends.

One honest limitation worth knowing before you build a project around it: because the .sf2 file itself stays local to your browser, it isn't embedded when you save or share a .tracks.json project file. If you hand that project file to someone else, they'll need to load the same SoundFont bank on their end for the SoundFont tracks to sound right. Bounced audio exports (WAV/MP3) aren't affected by this — once you export, the sound is baked into the file the same as any other track.

When to reach for a SoundFont instead of a built-in synth

Tracks' subtractive and FM synths are built for synthesized tones — basses, leads, pads, the sounds you shape from a waveform and a filter. A SoundFont is the opposite approach: it's a recording of a real instrument being played, sliced and mapped across the keyboard, so what you hear is closer to an actual piano, cello, or trumpet than anything a synth engine approximates from scratch.

A practical way to decide:

You wantReach for
A bass, lead, or pad you can shape with a filter and envelopeThe built-in subtractive or FM synth
A punchy kick, snare, or hat patternThe 808 or 909 drum machine
A chopped vocal, field recording, or one-shot you recorded yourselfThe 16-pad sampler
A real piano, string section, brass stab, or General MIDI-style instrumentA SoundFont bank

Building a part with a SoundFont, step by step

Step 1: Add a SoundFont track

In the arrange view, use the + SoundFont button to create a track set up for SoundFont playback.

Step 2: Load a .sf2 bank

Click Load .sf2 on that track and choose a SoundFont file from your device. Tracks parses it and shows you how many presets and samples it contains, confirming it loaded correctly.

Step 3: Pick a preset

Open the preset dropdown and choose the instrument you want from the bank. A General MIDI bank will typically give you a full set — grand piano, electric piano, strings, brass, organ, guitar, and more — all in one file.

Step 4: Write or play the part

Open the piano roll and draw in a melody or chord progression, or play it live if you have a MIDI keyboard connected — see how to use a MIDI keyboard in a browser DAW. The SoundFont track responds to velocity the same way a synth track does, so how hard you play (or draw) a note affects its tone and volume.

Step 5: Mix it in

Treat the track like anything else on the timeline — EQ it, compress it if it needs leveling, and send it to reverb or delay to place it in the mix. A real piano sample usually needs less shaping than a synth patch to sit well, but the same channel strip tools are there if it does.

Where to find SoundFont banks

Because the format is open and decades old, free .sf2 banks are easy to find with a search for "free SoundFont" or "General MIDI SF2" — orchestral banks, single-instrument banks (just a piano, just a guitar), and full GM sets covering 128 instruments are all common. Bank sizes vary widely, from a few megabytes for a single instrument to a few hundred megabytes for a large multisampled orchestral set. Since everything runs in your browser, a smaller bank will load and respond faster than a huge one — worth keeping in mind if you're on a slower connection or an older machine.

Frequently asked questions

Does Tracks come with any SoundFont banks built in?

No. Tracks provides the playback engine — the ability to load, parse, and play a .sf2 file accurately — but you bring your own bank. This keeps the studio itself lightweight and lets you use whichever SoundFont actually has the sound you want.

Is my SoundFont file uploaded anywhere?

No. The file is read and stored locally in your browser. Tracks never uploads your project or your sample banks to a server — see where does your project actually live? for how local storage works across the whole studio.

Can I use more than one SoundFont in the same song?

Yes. Each SoundFont track loads its own bank independently, so you could have a piano from one bank and strings from another on different tracks in the same project.

What happens if I share my project file with someone who doesn't have the same SoundFont?

The SoundFont tracks won't play back correctly until they load the same .sf2 file, since the bank isn't embedded in the project file. If you want to share a finished song with someone regardless of what's installed on their end, export it to WAV or MP3 instead — the audio is baked in and plays anywhere.

Do SoundFont tracks work with MIDI export?

Yes — the notes you write are standard note data, so they export the same way notes from any other track do. See how to export a MIDI file from your browser DAW.
