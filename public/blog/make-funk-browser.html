How to make funk music in your browser

By Tongkit Leong · November 15, 2026 · 7 min read

Funk is a groove built on tight sixteenth-note drums, a syncopated bass line that hits hard on beat 1, and short, muted guitar chords over a single chord. You can program all of it in the Tracks browser studio for free, with no install and no account.

What makes funk sound like funk

  • The one. Every part locks onto beat 1 of the bar, and the groove leans on it.
  • Sixteenth-note subdivision. Hi-hats and guitar play a fast grid, while the notes you accent are sparse.
  • A syncopated bass line with short notes and plenty of rests. Silence is part of the sound.
  • One chord for a long time. Funk often stays on a single dominant seventh or ninth chord and gets its motion from rhythm, not harmony.
  • Short, dry sounds. Notes are clipped so the groove stays tight.

Tracks gives you what you need: a piano roll with velocity and swing, the 808 and 909 drum machines and a sampler, a subtractive and FM synth, SoundFont (.sf2) playback for guitars and brass, and a mixer with EQ and compression.

Step 1: Set the tempo and feel

Open the studio and set the BPM between 95 and 115. Funk feels busy but not rushed. Set the grid to 1/16 so you can place notes on every sixteenth. Light swing helps if you want a looser, more relaxed pocket, and the quantize and swing guide explains how the setting changes the feel.

Step 2: Program the drums

Open the drum machine and build a one-bar pattern:

  • Kick: on beat 1, plus one or two syncopated hits, for example the "a" of 2 and the "and" of 3.
  • Snare: on beats 2 and 4, at full velocity.
  • Closed hi-hat: on every sixteenth note, with accents on the beats and quieter notes in between.
  • Ghost-note snares: very quiet hits in the gaps around the backbeats.

The ghost notes are what make it feel played. Our ghost notes guide covers velocity values in detail, and hi-hat patterns shows how to vary the sixteenths.

Step 3: Write the bass line

Add a bass track and write a one-bar riff in the piano roll. Start with a strong note on beat 1, then leave a gap. Use short notes, a jump of an octave, and a few rests. A good starting riff in E: a long E on beat 1, a short E an octave up on the "and" of 2, a short G, then a quick walk back down to E.

Keep the notes short. Long, ringing bass notes blur the groove. If the bass and kick fight, read how to lock a bassline with the kick.

Step 4: Add chicken-scratch guitar chords

On an electric guitar SoundFont or an electric piano, write short, muted chord stabs. Use one dominant ninth chord such as E9 for the whole loop. Place the stabs on sixteenth notes that sit between the hi-hat accents, and keep each note very short, one sixteenth or less. Lower the velocity of a few so some hits sound like scrapes.

Step 5: Add a horn or keys line

Write a short, punchy riff on a brass SoundFont or a synth. Let it answer the bass line instead of playing over it: you play, the bass plays, you play again. Two or three notes per phrase is plenty. For a clavinet-style keys sound, try a synth with a fast decay and no sustain; the ADSR guide shows how.

Step 6: Mix for punch

  1. Set the faders first: kick and bass balanced, snare above them, guitar and horns lower.
  2. Add a high-pass filter on the guitar and keys so they stay thin and leave the low end to the bass.
  3. Use gentle compression on the drum group, with a medium attack so the hits keep their snap.
  4. Use a very short room reverb or none at all. Funk is dry.

When you are happy, export a WAV or MP3. The walkthrough is in How to export your song to MP3 or WAV. Your project stays in your browser the whole time, and Tracks never uploads it.

Frequently asked questions

What tempo is funk?

Most funk sits between 95 and 115 BPM in 4/4, with a sixteenth-note feel. Faster funk exists, but this range is where the pocket is easiest to find.

What chords are used in funk?

Dominant seventh and ninth chords, often just one for the whole loop, such as E9 or A7. Some songs add a second chord a fourth higher.

What is the one in funk?

It is the first beat of the bar. Funk players accent it heavily and build the rest of the pattern around it, usually with a rest right after.

Do I need a real bass or guitar?

No. You can write the bass line in the piano roll and use a SoundFont guitar or electric piano. Short notes and good velocity changes matter more than the exact instrument.
