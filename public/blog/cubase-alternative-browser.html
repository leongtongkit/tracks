A free Cubase alternative that runs in your browser

By Tongkit Leong · November 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Cubase is Steinberg's paid, full-featured studio for Windows and Mac, with VST plugin hosting and deep editing tools. Tracks is a free alternative that runs in a browser tab, with built-in synths, drum machines, a mixer, and recording, and no install or account.

The core difference: a paid desktop app vs. a free tab

Cubase is desktop software you buy in tiers and install on a Windows or Mac computer. It is a long-standing professional tool, and it is built around hosting third-party VST plugins and handling large sessions.

Tracks has no tiers. You open freetracks.app in a modern browser on Windows, Mac, Linux, or a Chromebook, and every feature is there from the first click. Nothing is gated, and nothing is uploaded: projects stay on your device.

Where Cubase is still the stronger tool

Be clear about what you would give up. Cubase hosts VST plugins, so you can load third-party instruments, sample libraries, and mastering effects. Tracks cannot: browsers do not host native VST or AU plugins, so its instruments and effects are built into the engine.

Cubase also goes deeper on scoring, surround and film work, large-session audio editing, and hardware integration with audio interfaces and control surfaces. If your income or workflow depends on those, a browser studio is not a replacement.

Where Tracks is the better starting point

No cost, no install, no account

You can sketch a full idea before deciding whether you need a paid DAW at all. There is no licence, no download, and no sign-in standing between you and a blank timeline.

Instruments are already inside

Tracks includes a subtractive synth, an FM synth, 808/909-style drum machines, a 16-pad sampler, and SoundFont (.sf2) playback. See the best free VST alternative instruments in the browser for what each one does.

A mixer that does the everyday jobs

Every track has a parametric EQ, compressor, gate, and de-esser, plus reverb and delay sends, group buses, sidechain ducking, and automation. For a walkthrough of using them, read how to mix a track: EQ, compression, and reverb basics.

Recording, MIDI, and export

Record from a microphone with a count-in, play notes from a MIDI keyboard over Web MIDI, edit in a piano roll with velocity, quantize, and swing, and export WAV, MP3, stems, MIDI, or a project file. Imported audio can be split into vocals, drums, bass, and other stems.

Which one should you use?

Choose Cubase if

You rely on third-party VST plugins, you do scoring or large multitrack editing, or you want an offline desktop app that does not depend on a browser.

Choose Tracks if

You want to start now, on any computer, without paying first. Tracks covers the instruments, mixing, recording, and export most people need to finish a song. To see that path end to end, read how to make a song in your browser for beginners.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free version of Cubase?

Steinberg has offered trial and entry-level editions at various times, but the full Cubase is a paid product. Check Steinberg's site for current options. Tracks is free with no trial period.

Can Tracks load VST plugins?

No. Browsers cannot host native VST plugins, so Tracks ships its instruments and effects built in.

Can I move a project between Cubase and Tracks?

Not directly, since the project formats differ. Export a standard MIDI file from Tracks (see how to export a MIDI file) to bring notes into Cubase, or export stems or a WAV mixdown to move finished audio.

Does Tracks work on a Chromebook or Linux?

Yes. Cubase does not run on either, while Tracks runs in any modern browser.

Can I finish a full song in Tracks?

Yes. You can arrange, record, mix, and export a finished song without leaving the tab. Whether it is enough depends on whether you need VST hosting or Cubase's specialist tools.
