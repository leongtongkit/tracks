How to make holiday music in your browser

By Tongkit Leong · November 28, 2026 · 7 min read

A holiday song is a simple major-key tune with a steady sleigh-bell rhythm, warm chords, and a bell or glockenspiel melody that repeats. You can build all of it in the Tracks browser studio for free, with no install and no account.

What makes music sound like the holidays

  • A major key. Bright, familiar chords such as C, F, G and Am give the warm, nostalgic feel.
  • Bells. Glockenspiel, celesta, and chimes carry the melody in the high register.
  • A steady jingle. Shakers, tambourine, or sleigh-bell samples play a light, even eighth-note pulse.
  • A simple, repeated tune. Holiday melodies move mostly by step and repeat, so they are easy to sing.
  • Space. The arrangement is gentle, so every part can be heard.

Tracks has what you need: a piano roll with velocity, drum machines and a 16-pad sampler for bell and shaker sounds, a synth, SoundFont (.sf2) playback for piano, strings and bells, and a mixer with EQ, compression, and reverb.

Step 1: Pick a tempo and key

Open the studio and set the BPM. Between 90 and 110 feels like a warm ballad, and 120 to 130 feels upbeat and playful. Stay in 4/4. Choose a major key, C or G are easy, because you can play the whole tune on the white keys of the piano roll. If you want a refresher on scales, read major and minor scales in the piano roll.

Step 2: Add a sleigh-bell rhythm

Make a pulse that sounds like jingling bells. Load a shaker or tambourine sample into the sampler, or use a closed hi-hat from a drum machine, and place a hit on every eighth note. Set the on-beat hits louder and the off-beats quieter in velocity so it swings gently instead of ticking like a clock.

Add a soft kick on beats 1 and 3 and, if you like, a light snare or clap on beats 2 and 4. Keep the drums quiet, since the bells and chords are the stars. The hi-hat patterns guide shows more ways to shape the pulse.

Step 3: Write the chords

Add a piano or strings track. A reliable progression in C is C, Am, F, G (I, vi, IV, V), one chord per bar. Hold each chord for the whole bar, or play it as soft quarter notes. Try F, G, C, C for a gentler, resolved ending to the phrase. For more ideas, see chord progressions in the piano roll.

Step 4: Write a bell melody

Add a glockenspiel, celesta, or music-box SoundFont track and write a short tune in the high register. Use these rules:

  • Move mostly by step: neighbouring notes on the scale, with an occasional jump of a third or fifth.
  • Start and end phrases on a note from the current chord.
  • Use a rhythm of quarter notes and eighth notes, with a longer note at the end of the phrase.
  • Repeat the phrase, then change the last two or three notes.

Repetition is what makes it memorable. The catchy hook guide explains why.

Step 5: Add warmth and sparkle

Layer a soft pad or string SoundFont under the chords so the arrangement feels full. If you want a synth pad, the warm pad guide covers the settings. Then add a few high, quiet bell notes on beat 1 of each bar as a sparkle. Keep them short and light so they do not compete with the melody.

Step 6: Mix and export

  1. Set the faders first: melody on top, chords just under it, rhythm quietest.
  2. Use a high-pass filter on the bells and pad so the low end stays clear.
  3. Add a short-to-medium reverb to the bells for shimmer, and keep it off the rhythm.
  4. Check the mix at a low volume. If you can still hear the melody, it is balanced.

When you are happy, export a WAV or MP3. The walkthrough is in How to export your song to MP3 or WAV. Your project stays in your browser the whole time, and Tracks never uploads it.

Frequently asked questions

What key is best for a Christmas song?

A major key. C and G are the easiest to write, since the scale uses only the white keys in C. Major chords give the bright, warm sound people associate with the holidays.

What instruments sound like Christmas?

Glockenspiel, celesta, music box, sleigh bells, tambourine, strings, and piano. In Tracks you can use a SoundFont for the bells and strings and the sampler for jingle sounds.

What tempo should a holiday song be?

Use 90 to 110 BPM for a slow, cozy feel and 120 to 130 BPM for a lively one. Both work well in 4/4.

Can I use a popular carol in my song?

Traditional carols in the public domain are free to use, but a modern recording or arrangement may be protected. Writing your own melody avoids the question, and our music ownership guide explains the basics.
